AEPS (AADHAAR ENABLED PAYMENT SYSTEM)
----------------------------------------------------------------

What is AEPS:
AEPS is a payment system built by NPCI that allows any Indian citizen to perform basic banking transactions using only their Aadhaar number and biometric authentication. No debit card, no PIN, no smartphone, no internet connection is needed on the customer side.

Why AEPS was built:
India has over 190 million unbanked or underbanked citizens, primarily in rural areas. These people have bank accounts (Jan Dhan accounts) but no debit cards and no smartphones. AEPS was built to bring banking to their doorstep through local agents equipped with a biometric scanner and a micro-ATM device.

How AEPS works — step by step technical flow:
Step 1: Customer walks into a local agent shop (RapiPay agent, Spice Money agent).
Step 2: Customer provides their Aadhaar number verbally or on a keypad.
Step 3: Agent's micro-ATM device captures the customer's fingerprint using a biometric scanner.
Step 4: The device sends an encrypted authentication request to the acquiring bank.
Step 5: Acquiring bank forwards the request to NPCI AEPS switch.
Step 6: NPCI AEPS switch forwards biometric data to UIDAI (Unique Identification Authority of India) for authentication.
Step 7: UIDAI matches the fingerprint against their database and sends back an authentication result.
Step 8: NPCI routes the transaction to the customer's issuing bank (wherever their Aadhaar-linked account is).
Step 9: Issuing bank debits the customer's account.
Step 10: Confirmation flows back through NPCI to acquiring bank to agent device.
Step 11: Agent hands over cash to customer.

Transactions supported by AEPS:
- Cash Withdrawal: Customer withdraws cash from their bank account at agent location.
- Balance Enquiry: Customer checks their account balance.
- Mini Statement: Last 5 transactions of the account.
- Aadhaar to Aadhaar Fund Transfer: Transfer money from one Aadhaar-linked account to another.
- Cash Deposit: Some agents support depositing cash into customer account.

Technical limits:
- Maximum withdrawal per transaction: Rs 10,000 (varies by bank).
- Maximum transactions per day per customer: 3 to 5 depending on bank.
- Authentication timeout: UIDAI must respond within 3 seconds or transaction is declined.

Engineering considerations for AEPS:
- Biometric data (fingerprint template) must be encrypted using UIDAI-specified encryption before transmission. AES-256 is mandatory.
- Full Aadhaar number must never be stored. Only last 4 digits stored for reference (VID — Virtual ID should be used instead).
- Idempotency is critical. Same biometric request sent twice due to network retry must not result in double debit. Redis-based idempotency with unique request ID and TTL of 5 minutes is standard.
- Agent device must have a valid SSL certificate. Communication channel must be TLS 1.2 or higher.
- Transaction logs must capture: Aadhaar last 4, agent ID, device ID, timestamp, amount, bank reference number, UIDAI authentication reference number.
- UIDAI SLA: Authentication response within 3 seconds. If timeout, transaction must be marked FAILED and not retried automatically. Manual reconciliation required for ambiguous cases.
- Fallback handling: If UIDAI is down, AEPS transactions cannot proceed. Agent app must show clear error. No fallback to PIN-based AEPS — that does not exist.

Compliance requirements:
- UIDAI Circular compliance mandatory — defines data storage, encryption, and audit requirements.
- RBI Business Correspondent guidelines — agents operating AEPS must be registered as Banking Correspondents.
- Aadhaar Act compliance — prohibits storing biometric data. Only authentication response (yes/no) is retained, never the biometric template.

DMT (DOMESTIC MONEY TRANSFER)
----------------------------------------------------------------

What is DMT:
DMT allows any person to send money from their bank account to any other bank account in India instantly using just the recipient's mobile number or bank account number and IFSC code. Primarily used by migrant workers to send money to their families in home states.

Why DMT exists:
Migrant workers in cities like Mumbai, Delhi, Surat often do not have smartphones or internet banking. They earn daily wages in cash. DMT allows them to walk into a local agent shop, hand over cash, and the agent sends money digitally to their family account within seconds.

How DMT works — technical flow:
Step 1: Sender walks into agent shop, provides receiver's mobile number or account + IFSC.
Step 2: Agent verifies sender identity (OTP on registered mobile or biometric).
Step 3: Agent's system calls the DMT service provider API (RapiPay, Fino, etc.) with transfer details.
Step 4: DMT provider routes transaction via IMPS through NPCI.
Step 5: NPCI routes to receiver's bank.
Step 6: Receiver's bank credits the account.
Step 7: Confirmation sent back — agent app shows success and prints receipt.
Step 8: Agent collects cash from sender plus commission.

Transaction limits (as per RBI):
- Per transaction limit: Rs 25,000.
- Monthly limit per sender: Rs 1,00,000 (Rs 1 lakh).
- Daily limit: Varies by provider, typically Rs 50,000.
- KYC requirement: Basic KYC (name + mobile verification) for up to Rs 10,000 per month. Full KYC for higher limits.

Transaction statuses in DMT:
- INITIATED: Request received by DMT provider.
- PENDING: Request sent to NPCI/bank, waiting for response.
- SUCCESS: Bank confirmed credit to receiver account.
- FAILED: Bank declined or NPCI returned failure.
- REFUND_INITIATED: Failure confirmed, refund to sender wallet started.
- REFUNDED: Money back in sender/agent wallet.

Engineering considerations for DMT:
- IMPS response can take up to 30 seconds. System must handle async response with webhook callback from bank.
- Agent wallet must be debited atomically before initiating IMPS. If transaction fails, wallet credited back immediately.
- Unique client reference number (CRN) must be generated per transaction for idempotency. Bank uses this for duplicate detection.
- Status polling API must be available for cases where webhook is delayed or missed.
- Refund reconciliation: If IMPS fails but bank does not confirm failure within 2 hours, transaction moves to PENDING_RECONCILIATION state. Manual review team resolves these.
- SMS notification sent to receiver's mobile on successful credit.
- Agent commission calculated per transaction and credited to agent account at end of day.
- Fraud checks: Unusual transaction patterns, new receiver accounts, large amounts must be flagged for review.

UPI (UNIFIED PAYMENTS INTERFACE)
----------------------------------------------------------------

What is UPI:
UPI is India's real-time payment system that enables instant bank-to-bank transfers 24x7x365 using a Virtual Payment Address (VPA) like yashveer@okaxis. Launched by NPCI in 2016. Today the largest retail payment system in the world by transaction volume.

How UPI works — technical flow:
Step 1: User opens UPI app (PhonePe, GPay, Paytm).
Step 2: User enters receiver's VPA or scans QR code.
Step 3: User enters UPI PIN (4 or 6 digit, set during registration).
Step 4: App sends encrypted payment request to PSP (Payment Service Provider — the bank behind the app).
Step 5: PSP validates and forwards to NPCI UPI switch.
Step 6: NPCI routes to sender's bank to debit.
Step 7: NPCI routes to receiver's bank to credit.
Step 8: Both banks confirm. NPCI sends success response.
Step 9: PSP notifies both sender and receiver apps.
Total time: 3 to 10 seconds typically.

UPI transaction types:
- P2P (Person to Person): Transfer between individuals.
- P2M (Person to Merchant): Pay at shops, online checkout.
- Collect Request: Merchant or individual requests money from someone.
- UPI AutoPay: Recurring payments — Netflix subscription, SIP, loan EMI.
- UPI Lite: Small value payments up to Rs 500 without PIN (offline capable).
- UPI One World: For foreigners visiting India — no Indian bank account needed.
- UPI 123PAY: For feature phone users — works via IVR or missed call.

UPI limits:
- Standard limit: Rs 1 lakh per transaction.
- Higher limits for specific categories: Rs 2 lakh for capital markets, Rs 5 lakh for tax payments, IPO.
- Daily limit: Varies by bank, typically Rs 1 to 5 lakh.

Engineering considerations for UPI:
- QR code contains VPA, merchant name, amount (optional), and merchant category code.
- Deep link format: upi://pay?pa=vpa&pn=name&am=amount&cu=INR
- Webhook from NPCI/PSP confirms final transaction status.
- UPI transaction ID (12-digit number) is the primary reference for reconciliation.
- Merchant must acknowledge payment within 30 seconds or NPCI marks it timed out.
- Refunds on UPI: Initiated via UPI refund API, typically processed within 5 to 7 business days.
- UPI PIN is stored as hash on bank server — never transmitted in plain text.

IMPS (IMMEDIATE PAYMENT SERVICE)
----------------------------------------------------------------

What is IMPS:
IMPS is a 24x7 real-time interbank fund transfer service. Works via mobile number + MMID (Mobile Money Identifier) or account number + IFSC. IMPS is the backbone that UPI and DMT use for actual fund movement.

Limits: Up to Rs 5 lakh per transaction.
Charges: Rs 5 to Rs 15 per transaction depending on amount slab (banks may waive).
Used by: Net banking, mobile banking, DMT providers, corporate payment systems.

Difference between UPI and IMPS:
UPI is the application layer built on top of IMPS/NEFT infrastructure. UPI provides VPA (friendly address), QR codes, collect flows, and better UX. IMPS is the underlying fund movement rail. When you pay via UPI, the actual debit and credit happens via IMPS.

Engineering note:
IMPS response comes with a transaction reference number (RRN — Retrieval Reference Number). This RRN is used for reconciliation and dispute resolution with banks.

----------------------------------------------------------------
NEFT (NATIONAL ELECTRONIC FUNDS TRANSFER)
----------------------------------------------------------------

What is NEFT:
NEFT is an electronic fund transfer system that operates in half-hourly batches throughout the day. Unlike IMPS/UPI which are instant, NEFT settles in the next available batch.
Available: 24x7 since December 2019.
Limit: No maximum limit (minimum Rs 1).
Used for: Large corporate payments, salary transfers, vendor payments.
Settlement: Funds reach receiver in the next NEFT batch — maximum 2 hours wait.

----------------------------------------------------------------
RTGS (REAL TIME GROSS SETTLEMENT)
----------------------------------------------------------------

What is RTGS:
RTGS is for large-value transactions where real-time settlement is mandatory. Each transaction settles individually and immediately (gross settlement — no batching).
Minimum amount: Rs 2 lakh.
Used by: Corporates, businesses for large vendor payments, real estate transactions.
Available: 24x7 since December 2020.
Engineering note: RTGS is used when same-day guaranteed settlement is required. Common in treasury operations and high-value B2B payments.

NACH (NATIONAL AUTOMATED CLEARING HOUSE)
----------------------------------------------------------------

What is NACH:
NACH is a bulk payment system for recurring transactions. It works on the concept of a mandate — customer gives one-time authorization, and the bank auto-debits on specified dates automatically.

Use cases:
- EMI deductions for loans and credit cards.
- SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) for mutual funds.
- Insurance premium auto-debit.
- Utility bill auto-pay.
- Salary credit to employee accounts.
- Government subsidy disbursements (DBT — Direct Benefit Transfer).

How NACH mandate works:
Step 1: Customer signs NACH mandate (physical or e-mandate via net banking/Aadhaar OTP).
Step 2: NBFC/bank submits mandate to NPCI NACH system.
Step 3: NPCI registers mandate with customer's bank.
Step 4: On due date, NBFC submits debit file to NPCI.
Step 5: NPCI debits customer accounts in bulk.
Step 6: Settlement happens same day.
Step 7: Success/failure report sent back to NBFC.

Engineering note:
NACH files follow a specific format (NACH debit/credit files). Spring Batch is ideal for processing these large files — reading thousands of records, validating, submitting to NPCI, processing response file.

BBPS (BHARAT BILL PAYMENT SYSTEM)
----------------------------------------------------------------

What is BBPS:
BBPS is a centralized interoperable bill payment platform operated by NPCI. Any customer can pay any registered biller through any BBPS-enabled app or agent.

Biller categories on BBPS:
- Electricity: BSES, Tata Power, MSEDCL.
- Water and municipal taxes.
- Gas: Indraprastha Gas, Mahanagar Gas.
- Broadband and landline: Airtel, Jio, BSNL.
- DTH: Tata Sky, Dish TV, Airtel DTH.
- Mobile postpaid: All operators.
- Loan EMI: Select banks and NBFCs.
- Insurance premium: LIC and private insurers.
- FASTag recharge.
- Housing society maintenance.
- Education fees.
- Hospital bills.
- Subscription services.

How BBPS works:
Step 1: Customer provides biller name and consumer number.
Step 2: BBPS fetches bill amount from biller system.
Step 3: Customer confirms and pays.
Step 4: BBPS processes payment and notifies biller.
Step 5: Biller updates customer record as paid.
Step 6: Customer receives confirmation and receipt.

Engineering note:
BBPS provides a standard API for all billers. Payment apps integrate once with BBPS and get access to all registered billers — no need to integrate with each biller separately.

FASTAG
----------------------------------------------------------------

What is FASTag:
RFID-based electronic toll collection system for national highways. A sticker tag is affixed on car windshield linked to a prepaid wallet or bank account. When car passes through toll plaza, toll amount auto-deducted without stopping.

Managed by: NPCI under NETC (National Electronic Toll Collection) program.
Mandatory since February 2021 for all four-wheelers in India.
Recharge via: Any UPI app, net banking, BBPS.

How FASTag transaction works:
Step 1: Car approaches toll booth.
Step 2: RFID reader at toll scans the FASTag on windshield.
Step 3: Tag ID sent to NETC switch (NPCI).
Step 4: NPCI debits linked wallet or bank account.
Step 5: Boom barrier opens within 2 seconds.
Step 6: Transaction confirmation SMS sent to registered mobile.

Engineering note:
Transaction must complete within 2 seconds for smooth traffic flow. Low balance alert triggered via SMS webhook when balance falls below threshold. Monthly statement available via BBPS or bank app.

NPCI — NATIONAL PAYMENTS CORPORATION OF INDIA
----------------------------------------------------------------

What is NPCI:
NPCI is the umbrella organization that owns, operates, and manages all retail payment infrastructure in India. It is a not-for-profit entity set up by RBI and Indian Banks Association. Every major digital payment in India flows through NPCI infrastructure.

NPCI Products complete list:
- UPI: Unified Payments Interface — real-time bank-to-bank payments.
- IMPS: Immediate Payment Service — 24x7 instant transfer backbone.
- AEPS: Aadhaar Enabled Payment System — biometric banking for rural India.
- RuPay: India's domestic card payment network — alternative to Visa and Mastercard.
- NACH: National Automated Clearing House — bulk recurring payments.
- NETC/FASTag: Electronic toll collection on highways.
- BBPS: Bharat Bill Payment System — centralized bill payments.
- NEFT: National Electronic Funds Transfer — batch fund transfer.
- CTS: Cheque Truncation System — digital cheque clearing.
- UPI One World: UPI for foreign tourists without Indian bank account.
- UPI Lite: Offline small value payments.
- CBDC: Central Bank Digital Currency infrastructure support.

NPCI's role in the payment ecosystem:
NPCI acts as the central switch and settlement body. When you pay via any UPI app, NPCI is in the middle routing, validating, and settling the transaction between banks. No direct bank-to-bank communication happens — everything goes through NPCI.

NPCI settlement:
Settlement between banks happens 3 times a day via NPCI. Even though payments appear instant to users, actual net settlement between banks happens in batches. NPCI maintains the net position of each bank and settles via RBI.

Engineering interface with NPCI:
Fintech companies do not connect to NPCI directly. They connect through a sponsor bank that is a NPCI member. The sponsor bank provides APIs for UPI, AEPS, IMPS integration. All API calls go sponsor bank → NPCI → destination bank.

----------------------------------------------------------------
COMPLETE TRANSACTION LIFECYCLE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Phase 1 — Initiation:
User submits payment request via app or agent. System validates input — account numbers, IFSC format, amount limits, daily limits. Fraud pre-check runs in real time. Unique transaction ID generated. Client reference number (CRN) validated for idempotency — reject if same CRN seen within TTL window. Transaction record created in DB with status INITIATED.

Phase 2 — Processing:
Request forwarded to payment network (NPCI via sponsor bank). System awaits response. Status updated to PENDING. Redis lock acquired to prevent concurrent processing of same transaction. Timeout set — if no response within 30 to 60 seconds, transaction moves to AMBIGUOUS state for reconciliation.

Phase 3 — Confirmation:
Bank/NPCI sends response via synchronous API call or async webhook. SUCCESS: Transaction record updated, agent/merchant wallet credited if applicable, notification triggered. FAILED: Transaction record updated to FAILED, refund initiated if money was debited, notification sent. AMBIGUOUS/TIMEOUT: Moved to manual reconciliation queue. Team investigates using bank reference number.

Phase 4 — Settlement:
Actual money movement between banks happens in settlement batches (3 times daily via NPCI). Merchant/agent receives net settlement to their bank account on T+1 basis. Settlement file generated, matched against transaction records. UTR (Unique Transaction Reference) number issued by RBI for each settlement.

Phase 5 — Reconciliation:
Nightly batch job compares internal transaction records vs bank statement vs NPCI settlement report. Mismatches flagged in 5 categories. Finance team reviews flagged transactions. Disputes raised with banks for unresolved mismatches.

----------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT EVERY TRANSACTION RECORD MUST CONTAIN
----------------------------------------------------------------

Identifiers:
- system_transaction_id: UUID generated by internal system.
- client_reference_number: ID sent by calling system, used for idempotency.
- bank_reference_number: Reference number returned by bank/NPCI.
- npci_transaction_id: NPCI's internal transaction ID (for UPI/IMPS).
- rrn: Retrieval Reference Number from NPCI for disputes.

Transaction details:
- transaction_type: AEPS / DMT / UPI / QR / IMPS / NEFT.
- amount: Decimal, stored in paise (smallest unit) to avoid floating point errors.
- currency: INR default.
- channel: MOBILE_APP / AGENT_APP / API / WEB.

Sender details:
- sender_account_number: Masked, last 4 digits only stored.
- sender_bank_ifsc.
- sender_mobile: Hashed.
- sender_aadhaar_last4: For AEPS transactions.
- agent_id: If transaction via agent.
- device_id: Device from which transaction originated.

Receiver details:
- receiver_account_number: Masked.
- receiver_bank_ifsc.
- receiver_mobile.
- receiver_vpa: For UPI transactions.

Status and timing:
- status: INITIATED / PENDING / SUCCESS / FAILED / AMBIGUOUS / REFUND_INITIATED / REFUNDED.
- initiated_at: Timestamp with timezone.
- updated_at: Last status change timestamp.
- completed_at: Final status timestamp.
- settlement_status: PENDING / SETTLED / FAILED.
- settlement_date: Date when funds hit merchant/agent account.
- settlement_utr: UTR number from RBI settlement.

Error information:
- error_code: Bank or NPCI error code.
- error_message: Human-readable error description.
- retry_count: Number of retry attempts.

Audit fields:
- created_by: System or user that initiated.
- ip_address: IP of originating request.
- user_agent: App version and device info.
- geo_location: Lat/Long if available (for fraud detection).

TYPES OF REPORTS IN FINTECH
----------------------------------------------------------------

MIS Report (Management Information System):
Generated daily, typically by 6 AM for previous day's transactions.
Contents: Total transactions count and value by type, success rate by channel, failure analysis by error code, agent-wise performance, regional performance, peak hour analysis, comparison with previous day and previous week.
Consumers: Business heads, regional managers, operations team.
Format: Excel and PDF. Delivered via email. Also available on dashboard.
Engineering: Spring Batch job scheduled at 2 AM. Queries transaction DB, aggregates, generates Excel using Apache POI, uploads to S3, triggers email via SES.

Reconciliation Report:
Generated nightly after bank statement arrives (typically 1 to 3 AM).
Contents: Matched transactions (internal vs bank), mismatches by category (amount mismatch, missing in bank, missing in system, duplicate, status conflict), total mismatch count and value, pending resolution items from previous days.
Consumers: Finance team, reconciliation team, operations.
Format: CSV for processing, Excel for review.
Engineering: Spring Batch reads bank statement file (CSV/Excel format from bank), loads internal transaction data from DB, compares using composite key (amount + timestamp + reference number), writes mismatch records to reconciliation table, generates report.

Settlement Report:
Generated after each settlement cycle (3 times daily or once daily depending on arrangement).
Contents per merchant/agent: Gross transactions, total fees deducted, net amount settled, settlement UTR number, settlement bank account details, individual transaction list.
Consumers: Merchants, agents, finance team.
Format: PDF for merchant, CSV for internal processing.

Chargeback and Dispute Report:
Updated daily with latest status of all disputed transactions.
Contents: Transaction ID, original amount, dispute raised date, dispute reason (unauthorized, not received, wrong amount), current status (open, resolved, rejected), amount reversed if applicable, expected resolution date.
Consumers: Customer support team, finance team.
Engineering: Dispute data received from banks via file or API. Matched against internal transaction records. Status updated in dispute management table.

Regulatory Reports:
Submitted to RBI, NPCI, FIU-IND as per regulatory requirements.
STR (Suspicious Transaction Report): Transactions flagged by fraud detection system above threshold. Submitted to FIU-IND.
CTR (Cash Transaction Report): Cash transactions above Rs 10 lakh in a day by same customer. Submitted to FIU-IND.
NPCI MIS: Transaction volume and value statistics submitted daily to NPCI.
RBI Reports: Monthly reports on wallet transactions, PPI outstanding balance, transaction trends.

Agent Performance Report:
Daily/weekly/monthly report on each agent's transaction volume, success rate, commission earned, complaints raised, downtime.
Used for: Agent incentive calculation, fraud detection (unusual patterns), agent ranking and rewards program.

----------------------------------------------------------------
SETTLEMENT IN FINTECH — DETAILED
----------------------------------------------------------------

What is settlement:
Settlement is the actual movement of money between banks. When you pay at a merchant, the merchant sees the payment instantly but actual money from your bank to merchant's bank moves in settlement cycles.

How settlement works:
Step 1: During the day, NPCI tracks all transactions — who paid whom.
Step 2: NPCI calculates net position — Bank A owes Bank B Rs X million, Bank B owes Bank C Rs Y million.
Step 3: NPCI netting — instead of moving full gross amounts, only net differences are settled.
Step 4: Settlement happens via RBI's RTGS system (guaranteed funds).
Step 5: Each bank receives or pays net difference.
Step 6: Banks then credit merchant accounts based on their own settlement cycles.

Settlement timelines:
- UPI: T+1 (next working day) for merchants.
- Cards: T+2 for credit card, T+1 for debit card typically.
- Wallets: T+1.
- AEPS: T+1.
- NACH: Same day (D+0) for approved mandates.

MDR (Merchant Discount Rate):
Fee charged to merchant for accepting digital payments. Example: Merchant receives Rs 98 on Rs 100 payment — Rs 2 is MDR. MDR split among: acquiring bank, issuing bank, payment network (Visa/RuPay), and payment processor. UPI P2M MDR is currently zero for most categories (government waived).

APACHE KAFKA IN FINTECH
----------------------------------------------------------------

Why Kafka is essential in payment systems:
Payment systems must handle sudden spikes — festival sales, salary day, IPO subscription. Kafka absorbs traffic spikes — producers write at full speed, consumers process at their own pace. No direct API call chains that can cascade fail.

Kafka topic design in a payment system:
payment.initiated — fired when payment request accepted.
payment.processing — fired when sent to bank/NPCI.
payment.success — fired when bank confirms success.
payment.failed — fired when bank confirms failure.
payment.ambiguous — fired when timeout or no response.
notification.sms — consumed by SMS service.
notification.push — consumed by push notification service.
notification.soundbox — consumed by soundbox service.

audit.events — all system events for compliance audit log.
fraud.alerts — flagged transactions for review.

Kafka consumer groups in fintech:
Multiple services consume same payment events independently:
- Notification service: Sends SMS/push on payment.success.
- Analytics service: Updates merchant dashboard metrics.
- Ledger service: Updates wallet balances.
- Audit service: Writes to immutable audit log.
All consume from same topic, each in their own consumer group — each gets every message independently.

Kafka exactly-once semantics:
In payments, processing a transaction twice is catastrophic. Kafka supports exactly-once transaction semantics — message produced and consumed exactly once even on failures. Used for critical payment events.

Kafka retention:
Payment events retained for 7 days minimum (configurable to 30 days). Allows replay — if notification service was down for 2 hours, it can replay missed events and send delayed notifications.

REDIS IN FINTECH
----------------------------------------------------------------

Redis use cases in payment systems:

Idempotency key storage:
Key: "payment:idempotency:{client_reference_number}"
Value: {transactionId, status, response}
TTL: 24 hours
Logic: Before processing any payment, check if key exists. If yes, return stored response — do not process again. If no, process and store result with TTL.

Rate limiting:
Key: "rate:limit:agent:{agentId}:{minute}"
Value: count (incremented per request)
TTL: 60 seconds
Logic: If count exceeds limit (e.g. 100 requests per minute), reject request with HTTP 429.

Session management:
Key: "session:{sessionId}"
Value: {userId, role, permissions, loginTime}
TTL: 15 minutes (extended on activity)
Logic: JWT token contains session ID. Server validates session exists in Redis on every request.

OTP storage:
Key: "otp:{mobile}:{purpose}"
Value: {otp_hash, attempts}
TTL: 5 minutes
Logic: OTP hashed before storage. On verification, compare hash. Increment attempts counter. Block after 3 failed attempts.

Caching:
Bank IFSC details (100,000+ IFSC codes) cached in Redis — avoid DB query on every DMT transaction.
Merchant configuration cached — avoid DB query on every QR payment.
Exchange rates cached with 5-minute TTL.

Distributed locks:
Key: "lock:transaction:{transactionId}"
Lock prevents two instances from processing same transaction simultaneously (race condition in microservices).
Lock TTL: 30 seconds (auto-release if process crashes).
Implementation: Redis SET NX (set if not exists) with expiry.

POSTGRESQL IN FINTECH
----------------------------------------------------------------

Why PostgreSQL over MySQL in fintech:
ACID compliance with better isolation levels. Partial indexes allow indexing specific subsets (e.g. only PENDING transactions). Materialized views for complex reporting queries. Better JSON support for storing flexible metadata. Row-level locking more granular. Advisory locks for distributed lock coordination.

Critical table designs:

transactions table:
id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
client_reference_number VARCHAR UNIQUE,
transaction_type VARCHAR,
amount BIGINT (stored in paise, not rupees),
status VARCHAR,
created_at TIMESTAMPTZ,
updated_at TIMESTAMPTZ,
INDEX on (status, created_at) for reconciliation queries,
INDEX on (agent_id, created_at) for agent dashboard queries,
INDEX on (client_reference_number) for idempotency check.

Partitioning:
Transaction table partitioned by month — transactions_2024_01, transactions_2024_02. Queries for specific month only scan that partition. Old partitions moved to cold storage. Critical for performance when table has billions of records.

Connection pooling:
PgBouncer used as connection pooler between application and PostgreSQL. PostgreSQL has limited concurrent connections (typically 100-200). Connection pooler maintains pool of persistent DB connections, reuses them across application instances.

Read replicas:
Reporting queries run against read replica — separate PostgreSQL instance that replicates from primary. Prevents reporting queries from slowing down payment processing on primary. Primary handles all writes. Replica handles all reads for dashboards and reports.

SPRING SECURITY IN FINTECH
----------------------------------------------------------------

Authentication layers in fintech:
Layer 1 — TLS: All traffic encrypted in transit. Mutual TLS for bank-facing APIs (both client and server present certificates).
Layer 2 — API Key: Static key identifying the calling system (merchant, agent app, bank).
Layer 3 — HMAC Signature: Request body hashed with shared secret. Prevents tampering. Bank verifies signature before processing.
Layer 4 — JWT Token: User-level authentication. Contains user ID, role, permissions. Short expiry (15 minutes). Refresh token for session extension.
Layer 5 — Transaction PIN/OTP: Additional factor for high-value transactions.

ROLE_AGENT: Can initiate transactions, view own transaction history, check wallet balance.
ROLE_MERCHANT: Can view own transactions, access settlement data.
ROLE_DISTRIBUTOR: Can view all agent transactions under them, manage agent credit.
ROLE_ADMIN: Full access, user management, system configuration.
ROLE_AUDITOR: Read-only access to all data for audit purposes.

SPRING BATCH IN FINTECH
----------------------------------------------------------------

Spring Batch is the standard for processing large financial files in fintech.

Reconciliation job structure:
Job: DailyReconciliationJob
  Step 1: LoadBankStatementStep
    Reader: FlatFileItemReader reads bank CSV line by line.
    Processor: Validates format, converts to internal model.
    Writer: JdbcBatchItemWriter inserts to staging table.
  Step 2: ReconcileTransactionsStep
    Reader: JdbcCursorItemReader reads staging records.
    Processor: Matches against internal transactions, determines match status.

Chunk size optimization:
Processing 1 million records in chunk size 100 means 10,000 DB commits. Chunk size 1000 means 1000 DB commits — 10x faster but uses more memory. Optimal chunk size based on available heap and DB capacity.

Parallel processing:
Multiple steps run in parallel using Spring Batch parallel step execution. Different bank statement files processed simultaneously. Reduces total reconciliation time from hours to minutes.

----------------------------------------------------------------
ENCRYPTION
----------------------------------------------------------------

Data in transit:
All APIs use HTTPS with TLS 1.2 minimum (TLS 1.3 preferred). Certificate pinning in mobile apps — rejects connections to servers with unexpected certificates (prevents man-in-middle attacks). No HTTP allowed even on internal network for sensitive APIs.

Data at rest:
Database fields containing sensitive data encrypted using AES-256. Encryption keys stored separately in AWS KMS (Key Management Service) or HSM (Hardware Security Module). Application retrieves decryption key at startup. Key rotation annually (or immediately if compromise suspected).

Card data:
PCI-DSS mandates no storage of full card number (PAN), CVV, or PIN. Only last 4 digits stored for reference. Full card data tokenized — token stored instead of actual card number. Token useless without access to tokenization vault.

Aadhaar data:
UIDAI prohibits storing biometric data (fingerprint template, iris scan) — only yes/no authentication result stored. Full Aadhaar number must not be stored — only last 4 digits or Virtual ID (VID). All Aadhaar-related API calls use encrypted channel with UIDAI-specified key pairs.

----------------------------------------------------------------
TOKENIZATION
----------------------------------------------------------------

How tokenization works:
Original card number: 4111 1111 1111 1111
Token: 9873 5421 7823 1234 (random, same format)
Token stored in merchant system. Original number stored in secure vault (operated by Visa, Mastercard, or certified token service provider). When transaction needed, merchant sends token, vault maps to original number for bank processing. Token is merchant-specific — stolen token cannot be used at different merchant.

Network tokenization:
Visa, Mastercard, and RuPay now provide network-level tokens. Device-specific token created when you add card to PhonePe or Paytm. Token used for payment. If phone is stolen, token can be remotely deactivated without cancelling the actual card.

----------------------------------------------------------------
FRAUD DETECTION
----------------------------------------------------------------

Real-time fraud scoring:
Every transaction scored in real time before processing. Score 0 to 100 (100 = highest fraud risk). Decision: Score below 30: auto approve. Score 30 to 70: additional factor (OTP). Score above 70: auto decline.

Fraud signals:
- Unusual amount: Customer who normally transacts Rs 500 suddenly tries Rs 50,000.
- New device: First time this device used for this account.
- New location: Transaction from city different from usual pattern.
- Velocity: 5 transactions in 2 minutes — unusual for normal user.
- Time: 3 AM transaction for an account that normally transacts 9 AM to 9 PM.
- Receiver: Receiver account flagged in fraud database.
- Multiple failed PINs: 2 failed attempts before this transaction.

ML model for fraud:
Historical labeled transactions (fraud/not fraud) used to train model. Features: transaction amount, time, location, device, receiver, velocity, account age, KYC level. Model deployed as microservice. Scores every transaction in under 100 milliseconds.

----------------------------------------------------------------
KYC (KNOW YOUR CUSTOMER)
----------------------------------------------------------------

KYC levels and what they unlock:
Minimum KYC (mobile verification only): Wallet up to Rs 10,000 balance, Rs 10,000 monthly transaction limit.
Basic KYC (name + mobile + any one ID): Rs 1 lakh wallet balance, Rs 50,000 monthly limit.
Full KYC (Aadhaar + PAN + video/in-person verification): Rs 2 lakh wallet balance, unlimited transactions, bank account eligible.

KYC methods:
eKYC via Aadhaar OTP: Customer enters Aadhaar number, receives OTP on registered mobile, shares basic details with consent. Instant, paperless. Requires UIDAI authentication license.
Video KYC: Customer does live video call with bank agent. Agent verifies ID documents in real time. Allowed by RBI since 2020. Eliminates branch visit. Conducted via regulated video platform with recording.
Physical KYC: Customer visits branch or agent with original documents. Traditional method. Still required for some high-value products.
PAN Verification: PAN number verified against Income Tax Department database via NSDL/UTI API. Confirms PAN is valid and belongs to the person.

----------------------------------------------------------------
PCI-DSS COMPLIANCE
----------------------------------------------------------------

PCI-DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard):
Mandatory for any organization that stores, processes, or transmits card data. 12 requirements covering:
1. Install and maintain firewall configuration.
2. No vendor-supplied default passwords.
3. Protect stored cardholder data (encryption, tokenization).
4. Encrypt transmission of cardholder data over open networks.
5. Use and update anti-virus software.
6. Develop and maintain secure systems and applications.
7. Restrict access to cardholder data by business need.
8. Assign unique ID to each person with computer access.
9. Restrict physical access to cardholder data.
10. Track and monitor all access to network resources and cardholder data.
11. Regularly test security systems and processes (penetration testing).
12. Maintain policy that addresses information security.

PCI-DSS audit: Conducted annually by Qualified Security Assessor (QSA). Failure means losing ability to process card payments.

----------------------------------------------------------------
RBI REGULATIONS AFFECTING FINTECH ENGINEERING
----------------------------------------------------------------

Data localization:
RBI mandates all payment data of Indian users must be stored in India. No payment transaction data can be stored or processed on servers outside India. Engineering: AWS region must be ap-south-1 (Mumbai). No cross-region replication of payment data to regions outside India.

Cooling period for new payee:
RBI mandates that for first-time payee added by a customer, there is a 30-minute cooling period before transaction can go through (above certain amount). Engineering: Store payee addition timestamp, reject transaction if within cooling period, notify customer.

Two-factor authentication:
All online transactions above Rs 2,000 require second factor (OTP). Engineering: OTP generated by bank, sent to registered mobile. Customer enters OTP in app. App forwards to bank for verification. Transaction proceeds only after OTP validation.

Transaction limit on new devices:
RBI mandates lower transaction limits when account accessed from new device for first 24 hours. Engineering: Track device registration date. Apply reduced limits in authorization logic for new devices.

Grievance redressal:
RBI mandates payment companies must resolve customer complaints within 30 days. Must maintain records of all complaints and resolutions. Engineering: Ticketing system integrated with transaction data. Automated escalation if not resolved within 7 days.

Audit trail retention:
RBI mandates transaction records retained for minimum 5 years. Engineering: Transaction data never hard deleted. Soft delete flag at most. Old data archived to S3 Glacier for cost-effective long-term storage. Retrievable on demand for regulatory audit.


================================================================
SECTION 6: INTERESTING ENGINEERING FACTS
================================================================

UPI scale that makes engineers proud:
UPI processed 13.89 billion transactions worth Rs 18.41 lakh crore in November 2023. That is roughly 5,341 transactions per second at average. On peak days like salary day or festival, peak can hit 10,000+ transactions per second. India's UPI processes more real-time transactions than the rest of the world combined. This is one of the greatest engineering achievements in financial technology globally.

Why floating point is banned for money:
0.1 + 0.2 = 0.30000000000000004 in IEEE 754 floating point. In a system processing 10 million transactions per day, even Re 0.01 error per transaction = Rs 1,00,000 per day discrepancy. All fintech systems store amounts in smallest denomination (paise, cents) as integers. Rs 100.50 stored as 10050 (paise). All arithmetic in integers. Convert to display format only for UI.

The reconciliation problem:
At peak, payment system processes thousands of transactions per second. Each transaction touches multiple systems: payment service, bank, NPCI, notification service, ledger. Network failures, timeouts, partial failures can cause inconsistent state across systems. Nightly reconciliation is not a nice-to-have — it is the safety net that ensures money is never lost. Without reconciliation, you cannot trust your own system's data.

Why idempotency is non-negotiable:
Mobile network drops. User taps Pay button. Request goes to server. Server processes, sends response. Response lost in network drop. App shows error. User taps Pay again. Without idempotency, customer charged twice. With idempotency key (Redis, 24-hour TTL): second request detected as duplicate, original response returned, no double charge. This is one of the most important engineering principles in payments.

The distributed systems challenge in payments:
Consistent state is impossible to guarantee across distributed systems during network partitions (CAP theorem). Fintech systems choose Availability + Partition Tolerance (AP) over Consistency. Transactions processed and confirmed (Available) even if not all nodes agree (not Consistent). Eventual consistency achieved via reconciliation. This is why your payment shows success on phone but bank deducts slightly later — systems converge to consistent state eventually.

Why zero downtime deployment is critical:
A payment gateway cannot go offline for 5 minutes for deployment. Thousands of transactions would fail. Blue-green deployment: Run old version and new version simultaneously. Route 5% traffic to new version. Monitor. Increase to 25%, 50%, 100% gradually. If issue found, route 100% back to old version in seconds. Zero user impact.

The soundbox engineering problem:
Soundbox must announce payment immediately when customer pays. The chain: Customer pays → NPCI confirms → Bank webhook → Payment service → Kafka event → Notification service → Soundbox service → Device. Total end-to-end latency must be under 3 seconds. Every hop adds latency. Kafka partitioning, consumer group sizing, and network optimization critical. Merchants lose trust if announcement is delayed — they cannot confirm payment.

Settlement float — how fintechs earn:
When 10 million users keep Rs 500 average in their Paytm wallet, that is Rs 500 crore sitting in Paytm's nodal account. Even at 4% annual interest, that is Rs 20 crore per year in float income. This is why wallets encourage users to keep money in wallet rather than transfer to bank. RBI regulates this — mandates a minimum percentage to be maintained in government securities.

Why last mile fintech is a hard engineering problem:
A rural AEPS transaction starts at a micro-ATM in a village with 2G connectivity (50-200 kbps). It goes to the acquiring bank, then UIDAI for biometric authentication, then NPCI, then the customer's bank which might be a cooperative bank with aging infrastructure, then back. Each hop has different SLAs and reliability. System must handle partial failures gracefully, retry intelligently, and never leave customer in ambiguous state (money deducted but transaction showing failed on device). This is harder than building a payment system for urban high-speed internet users.
